\section{Coverings and smooth structures}
A fundamental application of covering spaces is the classification of topological manifolds. Taking our cue from invariance of domain, which states that, given a Euclidean space $X$, there is a unique $n$ such that $X\cong\mathbb R^n$, we ask: is there a list of all possible $n$-manifolds, up to homeomorphism?

Covering-space theory aids us in answering this question because manifolds are semilocally simply connected and locally path connected. Thus, for every subgroup $H<\pi_1(M,m)$, covering-space classification provides a map $p:E\to M$ such that $p_*(\pi_1(E,e))=H$. Set $H=\{1\}$. The way $E$ is constructed for this $H$ forces $E$ to be simply connected. Moreover, $p:E\to M$ is a normal covering, so $M\cong E/G$, where $G<\Aut(E)$ is a subgroup acting properly discontinuously.

Therefore, covering-space theory has helped us reduce the classification problem for arbitrary topological manifolds to that of classifying simply connected manifolds. We now ask whether this construction is compatible with smooth structures. Two questions guide the discussion:
\begin{enumerate}[label=Q\arabic*.]
 \setcounter{enumi}{0}\item When is a topological covering map between smooth manifolds a smooth covering map?
% Source page 2
 \setcounter{enumi}{1}\item When is a smooth map between manifolds a covering map?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The lifted smooth structure}
\setcounter{definition}{0}\begin{definition}[Smooth covering]
A topological covering map $p:E\to M$ is said to be a \emph{smooth covering map} if, for every $x\in M$, there is an evenly covered neighbourhood $U_x$ of $x$ with
\[
 p^{-1}(U_x)=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in I}V_\alpha,
 \qquad p|_{V_\alpha}:V_\alpha\longrightarrow U_x
 \quad\text{a diffeomorphism for every }\alpha\in I.
\]
\end{definition}

\setcounter{theorem}{0}\begin{proposition}[Answer to Q1]\label{prop:lift-smooth}
Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-manifold, $E$ a connected topological space, and $p:E\to M$ a topological covering map. Then $E$ admits a unique smooth structure which makes it a smooth $n$-manifold and makes $p$ a smooth covering map.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Hausdorffness.} It is easy to see that $E$ is Hausdorff. Let $e_1,e_2\in E$. If $p(e_1)\ne p(e_2)$, then the Hausdorff property of $M$ separates $p(e_1)$ and $p(e_2)$ by neighbourhoods which can be pulled back to $E$. If $p(e_1)=p(e_2)$, then the covering-map definition separates $e_1$ and $e_2$: let $\widetilde p=p(e_1)=p(e_2)$ and let $U_{\widetilde p}$ be an evenly covered neighbourhood of $\widetilde p$. Then $e_1$ and $e_2$ must belong to different slices of $p^{-1}(U_{\widetilde p})$. Hence $E$ is Hausdorff.

% e_1,e_2 is the clear local label typo, as its next sentence explicitly states.

\end{figure}

\paragraph{Compatible charts.}
We now equip $E$ with a smooth structure. Let $y\in E$, put $x=p(y)$, and let $U_x$ be an evenly covered neighbourhood of $x$. Let $(\varphi,U'_x)$ be a chart containing $x$, so $x\in U'_x$. Shrink $U_x$ (or $U'_x$) to $U_x\cap U'_x=U$. Then $(\varphi,U)$ is still a chart, and $U$ is still an evenly covered neighbourhood of $x$.

Write $p^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in I}V_\alpha$. Say $y\in V_\alpha$; then $(V_\alpha,\varphi\circ p|_{V_\alpha})$ is a chart for $E$. These charts are compatible. Let $(\widetilde V,\widetilde\varphi)$ and $(\widetilde W,\widetilde\psi)$ be two such charts for $E$. The transition map is
\[
 \widetilde\psi\circ\widetilde\varphi^{-1}:
 \widetilde\varphi(\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W)
 \longrightarrow\widetilde\psi(\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W).
\]
Here $\widetilde\psi=\psi\circ p|_{\widetilde W}$ and $\widetilde\varphi=\varphi\circ p|_{\widetilde V}$, where $(p(\widetilde W),\psi)$ and $(p(\widetilde V),\varphi)$ are charts of $M$.
\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{sc-fig-02.pdf}
\end{figure}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde\psi\circ\widetilde\varphi^{-1}
 &=\left(\widetilde\psi|_{\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W}\right)
   \circ\widetilde\varphi^{-1}|_{\widetilde\varphi(\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W)}\\
 &=\left(\psi\circ p|_{\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W}\right)
   \circ\left(\varphi\circ p|_{\widetilde V}\right)^{-1}
   |_{\widetilde\varphi(\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W)}\\
 &=\left(\psi\circ p|_{\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W}\right)
   \circ\left(\varphi\circ p|_{\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W}\right)^{-1}\\
 &=\psi\circ p|_{\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W}
   \circ\left(p|_{\widetilde V\cap\widetilde W}\right)^{-1}\circ\varphi^{-1}
  =\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}.
\end{align*}

% Source page 4
\paragraph{Smoothness of the covering map.}
Another immediate fact is that, with this smooth structure, $p$ is a smooth map. Let $y\in E$ and $x=p(y)$, let $(\varphi,U)$ be the evenly covered chart of $M$, and let $(\widetilde\varphi,V)$ be the corresponding chart of $E$, as constructed before. Then $p(V)\subseteq U$; in fact, $p(V)=U$. We have
\[
 \varphi\circ p\circ\widetilde\varphi^{-1}
 =\varphi\circ p\circ(\varphi\circ p)^{-1}=\id,
\]
which is smooth from $\widetilde\varphi(V)$ to $\varphi(U)$. Thus $p$ is a smooth covering map. What we have in particular managed to prove here is that $p|_V:V\to U$ is smooth; one can similarly prove that the inverse branch $p^{-1}|_U:U\to V$ is smooth.



\paragraph{Uniqueness.}
It remains to prove uniqueness. For this purpose, restrict the relevant open sets. Let $E$ and $E'$ denote the topological space $E$ with two smooth structures, of which $E$ is the one we constructed. In order to show $E\sim E'$, meaning that these two structures are the same, one must show that charts of $E'$ and charts of $E$ are compatible.

% Source page 5
Let us now consider the following diagram. Here $E$ and $E'$ denote the same topological space $E$, but with different smooth structures.
\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{sc-fig-03.pdf}
\end{figure}
Let $E$ be the manifold we constructed. There is a continuous lift $\widetilde p$ of $p$, since $E=E'$ as topological manifolds and $\pi_1(E)=\pi_1(E')$.

We shall first show that $\widetilde p$ is smooth. Let $(\psi,V)$ be a chart at $x\in M$ such that $V$ is an evenly covered neighbourhood of $x$, and let $U$ be such that $p'|_U:U\to V$ is a diffeomorphism. Let $(\sigma,W)$ be a chart at $y\in E$ such that $\widetilde p(W)\subseteq U$. Then
\[
 \psi\circ(p'\circ\widetilde p)\circ\sigma^{-1}
 \text{ is smooth}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 (\psi\circ p')\circ\widetilde p\circ\sigma^{-1}
 \text{ is smooth}.
\]
Hence $\widetilde p$ is smooth, using the charts $(\sigma,W)$ and $(\psi\circ p',U)$.

Now fix a point $m\in M$, and let $e$ be a point of the fibre over $m$, viewed as the same underlying point of $E$ and $E'$. Let $\widetilde p$ be the unique lift fixing $e$. One can similarly construct a lift of $p'$ that fixes $e$. Notice that $\id_E$ and $\widetilde p'\circ\widetilde p$ are both lifts of $p$, and they agree at a fixed basepoint.
\begin{figure}[H]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{sc-fig-04.pdf}
\end{figure}
It follows that $\id_E=\widetilde p'\circ\widetilde p$ and $\id_{E'}=\widetilde p\circ\widetilde p'$. Therefore $\widetilde p$ is a diffeomorphism. Further, $\widetilde p=\id_E$, since $p'=p$ as topological maps. Thus the smooth structures of $E$ and $E'$ are compatible.

% Source page 6
\paragraph{Second countability.}
Now let us show that $E$ is a topological manifold. It remains to prove that $E$ is second countable. Notice that second countability allowed us to construct a countable partition of unity, with which we constructed smooth exhaustion functions and level sets, which is used throughout submanifold theory. Here is how we shall prove that $E$ is second countable.

Consider the monodromy action of $\pi_1(M,q)$ on the fibre $p^{-1}(q)$, given by $P_0\mapsto f\cdot P_0=\widetilde f(1)$, where $\widetilde f(0)=P_0$. Fix $P_0\in p^{-1}(q)$ and define
\[
 \Phi:\pi_1(M,q)\longrightarrow p^{-1}(q),
 \qquad \Phi([f])=f\cdot P_0.
\]
If $E$ is connected, it is path connected, so $\Phi$ is surjective. Now, if we prove that $\pi_1(M,q)$ is countable, then the rest follows. Since $M$ is second countable, it is Lindel\"of. If $\{U_x\}_{x\in M}$ is the cover of $M$ by evenly covered neighbourhoods, a countable subcover $\{U_i\}_{i=1}^{\infty}$ exists. Countability of $\pi_1(M,q)$ implies that $p^{-1}(q)$ is countable. Consequently, $p^{-1}(U_i)$ has countably many slices, and each slice, being diffeomorphic to $U_i$, is second countable.

Thus, by taking the union of the countable bases of $\{p^{-1}(U_i)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}$, one obtains a countable basis of $E$. The following lemma will finish our proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Countability of the fundamental group}
\setcounter{theorem}{1}\begin{lemma}[Countability of the fundamental group]\label{lem:countable-fundamental}
Let $M$ be a topological manifold. Then $\pi_1(M,P)$ is countable for every $P\in M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\{B_i\}_{i=1}^{\infty}$ be a countable basis of coordinate balls. Since $M$ is second countable, each $B_i\cap B_j$ has only countably many components. Pick a point $x_i\in\widehat{B_j\cap B_k}$, where $\widehat{B_j\cap B_k}$ varies over all components of all intersections $B_j\cap B_k$, and let $X=\{x_i\}$. Notice that $P\in M$ belongs to a component of some $B_j\cap B_k$, so we may assume that $P\in X$.

Let $[f]\in\pi_1(M,P)$, with $f\colon[0,1]\to M$. By the Lebesgue number lemma, there is a partition $0=a_0<a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_n=1$ of $[0,1]$ such that $f([a_{i-1},a_i])\subseteq B_i$ for suitable basis elements. Notice that $f(a_i)\in B_i\cap B_{i+1}$ for $1\leq i<n$; let $x_i$ and $f(a_i)$ lie in the same component, and set $x_0=x_n=P$. Thus $f=f_1*f_2*\cdots*f_n$, where $f_i=f|_{[a_{i-1},a_i]}$ and $*$ denotes the usual concatenation.

Let $g_0$ and $g_n$ be the constant path at $P$, and, for $1\leq i<n$, let $g_i$ be a path from $x_i$ to $f(a_i)$. We can then factor $f$ as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
f&=\underbrace{g_0*f_1*g_1^{-1}}_{h_1}
  *\underbrace{g_1*f_2*g_2^{-1}}_{h_2}
  *\cdots*\underbrace{g_{n-1}*f_n*g_n^{-1}}_{h_n}\\
 &=h_1*h_2*\cdots*h_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{sc-fig-05.pdf}
\end{figure}

% Source page 8
\Needspace{6.6cm}
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{sc-fig-06.pdf}
\end{figure}

Thus every $f$ can be factored using paths $h_{x_{i-1}x_i}$ from $x_{i-1}$ to $x_i$. Notice that both $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ belong to $B_i$. Hence any two paths $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ between them satisfy $\gamma_1\simeq\gamma_2$. It follows that
\[
[f]=\mathop{*}_{i=1}^{n}[h_{x_{i-1}x_i}].
\]
Every $[f]\in\pi_1(M,P)$ is therefore a finite product of elements from the countable set $\{[h_{x_{i-1}x_i}]\}_{n,i}$. Consequently, $\pi_1(M,P)$ is countable.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proper local diffeomorphisms}

\setcounter{theorem}{2}\begin{proposition}[Answer to Q2]\label{prop:proper-cover}
Let $M$ be a connected smooth manifold and let $p\colon E\to M$ be a proper local diffeomorphism. Then $p$ is a covering map.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The image $p(E)$ is open because $p$ is a local diffeomorphism. It is also closed because $p$ is proper and $M$ is locally compact and Hausdorff. Since $M$ is connected, $p$ is surjective.

% Source page 9
Since $p$ is a local diffeomorphism, $p^{-1}(\{q\})$ is discrete. It is also compact, because $p$ is proper, and hence is finite. Write $p^{-1}(q)=\{p_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$. For each pair $p_i,q$, there are open neighbourhoods $V_i$ and $U_i$ of $p_i$ and $q$, respectively, such that $p|_{V_i}\colon V_i\to U_i$ is a diffeomorphism.

Since $E$ is Hausdorff, there are neighbourhoods $W_i$ of $p_i$ such that $W_i\cap W_j=\varnothing$ whenever $i\ne j$. Replace $V_i$ by $V_i\cap W_i$ and $U_i$ by $p|_{V_i}(V_i\cap W_i)$. The restriction $p|_{V_i}\colon V_i\to U_i$ is again a diffeomorphism.

Now replace each $U_i$ by $U=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}U_i$ and each $V_i$ by $V_i\cap(p|_{V_i})^{-1}(U)$. Then $p|_{V_i}\colon V_i\to U$ is a diffeomorphism for every $i$, and $V_i\cap V_j=\varnothing$ for $i\ne j$.

The set $K=\bigl(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_i\bigr)^c$ is closed in $E$, so $p(K)$ is closed. Replace $U$ by $U\setminus p(K)$.

% Source page 10
Then $p^{-1}(U)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V_i$. Finally, replace $V_i$ by $V_i\cap p^{-1}(U)$. After these restrictions, we have
\[
p^{-1}(U)=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}V_i,
\qquad p|_{V_i}\colon V_i\longrightarrow U
\quad\text{a diffeomorphism}.
\]
Thus $p$ is a smooth covering map.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Covering Lie groups}

The lifted-smooth-structure result extends naturally to Lie groups: the universal cover of a connected Lie group inherits a compatible Lie-group structure.

\setcounter{theorem}{3}\begin{proposition}[Lifting the group structure]\label{prop:lie-group}
Let $G$ be a connected Lie group, and let $\pi\colon\widetilde{G}\to G$ be a smooth covering map with $\widetilde{G}$ simply connected. Then one can endow $\widetilde{G}$ with a Lie group structure for which $\pi$ is a Lie group homomorphism. Furthermore, $\widetilde{G}$ is unique up to Lie group isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

% Source page 11
\begin{proof}
Consider $\widetilde G$, the universal cover of $G$. Then
$\pi\colon\widetilde G\to G$ is a smooth covering map. Therefore,
$\pi\times\pi\colon\widetilde G\times\widetilde G\to G\times G$ is also a
smooth covering map: one can evenly cover $G\times G$ componentwise. There
is a multiplication map on $G\times G$; let's lift it.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{sc-fig-07.pdf}
\end{figure}

The diagram gives $\pi\circ\widetilde m=m\circ(\pi\times\pi)$, or
$\pi(\widetilde m(x,y))=\pi(x)\cdot\pi(y)$. Let $\widetilde m$ be the lift
of $m\circ(\pi\times\pi)$. For $x,y\in\widetilde G$, define
$x\cdot y:=\widetilde m(x,y)$. Notice now that $\pi$ preserves
multiplication. We know that $m\circ(\pi\times\pi)$ is continuous, hence
so is its lift. Also, $\pi\circ\widetilde m$, being equal to
$m\circ(\pi\times\pi)$, is smooth; thus $\widetilde m$ is smooth.

(Indeed, $\pi$ is a local diffeomorphism. Let
$\pi\colon V_p\to U_{\pi(p)}$ be the diffeomorphism between neighbourhoods
of $p$ and $\pi(p)$. Since $\pi\circ\widetilde m$ is smooth, there are
neighbourhood charts $(W,\varphi)$ at $q\in\widetilde m^{-1}(p)$ and
$(U_{\pi(p)},\psi)$ at $\pi(p)$ such that
$\psi\circ(\pi\circ\widetilde m)\circ\varphi^{-1}$ is smooth. One can
arrange $U_{\pi(p)}$ so that $\pi\colon V_p\to U_{\pi(p)}$ is a local
diffeomorphism. Then $(W,\varphi)$ and $(V_p,\psi\circ\pi)$ are charts
such that $(\psi\circ\pi)\circ\widetilde m\circ\varphi^{-1}$ is smooth.
This can be done for every $q$ and $\pi(p)$.

% Source page 12
Therefore, $\widetilde m$ is smooth.) Thus one has a smooth
multiplication function.

Similarly, by lifting $i\circ\pi$, where $i$ is the inverse map on $G$,
one gets a smooth inverse map $\widetilde i$ on $\widetilde G$. Now it
remains to check that $\widetilde G$ indeed forms a group with
$\widetilde m$ and $\widetilde i$ as its operations.

Pick $\widetilde e\in\pi^{-1}(e)$. One can arrange $\widetilde m$ and
$\widetilde i$ so that $\widetilde m(\widetilde e,\widetilde e)=\widetilde e$
and $\widetilde i(\widetilde e)=\widetilde e$. These choices are unique. Let $f\colon\widetilde G\to\widetilde G$ be
defined by $f(x)=\widetilde e\cdot x$. Then
\[
  (\pi\circ f)(x)=\pi(\widetilde e\cdot x)
  =\pi(\widetilde e)\cdot\pi(x)=\pi(x).
\]
Now $f$ and $\operatorname{Id}_{\widetilde G}$ are both lifts of $\pi$,
and $f(\widetilde e)=\widetilde e\cdot\widetilde e=\widetilde e
=\operatorname{Id}_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde e)$. Hence
$f=\operatorname{Id}_{\widetilde G}$, so $\widetilde e\cdot x=x$.
The same argument, applied to the map $x\mapsto x\cdot\widetilde e$, gives
$x\cdot\widetilde e=x$.

Now let's move on to associativity. Define
$\alpha_L,\alpha_R\colon\widetilde G\times\widetilde G\times\widetilde G
\to\widetilde G$ by
\[
  \alpha_L(x,y,z):=(x\cdot y)\cdot z,
  \qquad \alpha_R(x,y,z):=x\cdot(y\cdot z).
\]
Note that $(\pi\circ\alpha_L)(x,y,z)=(\pi\circ\alpha_R)(x,y,z)
=\pi(x)\pi(y)\pi(z)$. Thus $\alpha_L$ and $\alpha_R$ are lifts of
$\pi(x)\pi(y)\pi(z)$, and they agree at the base point
$(\widetilde e,\widetilde e,\widetilde e)$. Since the two lifts agree at the base point, uniqueness of lifts gives $\alpha_L=\alpha_R$, proving associativity.

% Source page 13
One can similarly show that $x\cdot x^{-1}=x^{-1}\cdot x=\widetilde e$.
Therefore $(\widetilde G,\cdot)$ is a Lie group and
$\pi\colon\widetilde G\to G$ is a Lie group homomorphism.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniqueness across the categories}

Now let's briefly talk about the uniqueness of $\widetilde G$, to see how
the structures are lifted from one category to another. If $G_1$ and
$G_2$ are two simply connected Lie groups with smooth coverings
$p_1\colon G_1\to G$ and $p_2\colon G_2\to G$ that are also Lie group
homomorphisms, then we have the following.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{sc-fig-08.pdf}
\end{figure}

There are continuous lifts $\widetilde p_1$ and $\widetilde p_2$ of
$p_1$ and $p_2$ such that the diagram commutes. Thus $\widetilde p_2$ is
a homeomorphism. Next, $p_1\circ\widetilde p_2$ is smooth and
$\widetilde p_2$ is continuous, with $p_1$ a local diffeomorphism. Hence
$\widetilde p_2$ is smooth, and therefore a diffeomorphism.

% Source page 14
Notice that
\begin{align*}
 p_1\bigl(\widetilde p_2(xy)\bigr)
   &=p_2(xy)=p_2(x)\cdot p_2(y)\\
   &=p_1\bigl(\widetilde p_2(x)\bigr)\cdot
     p_1\bigl(\widetilde p_2(y)\bigr)
    =p_1\bigl(\widetilde p_2(x)\cdot\widetilde p_2(y)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Hence $\widetilde p_2\circ m_2$ and
$m_1\circ(\widetilde p_2\times\widetilde p_2)$ are lifts of the same map
$m\circ(p_2\times p_2)$. Here $m_2$ and $m_1$ denote the multiplication
maps of $G_2$ and $G_1$, respectively. If we show that
$\widetilde p_2\circ m_2$ and
$m_1\circ(\widetilde p_2\times\widetilde p_2)$ agree at a base point,
say $(e_2,e_2)$, where $e_2$ is the identity of $G_2$, then uniqueness
of lifts would tell us that
$\widetilde p_2(xy)=\widetilde p_2(x)\widetilde p_2(y)$.

Choose the unique lift $\widetilde p_2$ of $p_2$ through $p_1$ such that
$\widetilde p_2(e_2)=e_1$, where $e_1$ is the identity of $G_1$. For that $\widetilde p_2$, one can similarly
prove $\widetilde p_2(x^{-1})=(\widetilde p_2(x))^{-1}$. Thus
$\widetilde p_2$ is a group homomorphism, hence a group isomorphism,
and therefore, of course, a covering map.

\section{Classification and complex structures}

We didn't impose the Lie group structure simply to poke around and see.
It turns out that the initial simplification of classifying topological
manifolds, when translated to the category of smooth manifolds, would
pertain to classifying all simply connected smooth manifolds and their
groups of automorphisms. One finds that $G<\operatorname{Aut}(E)$, acting
properly discontinuously on $E$ to yield all manifolds, inherits a discrete Lie-group structure. Most often, for some specific $E$, the
group $\operatorname{Aut}(E)$ is a Lie group. Thus our original
classification problem boils down to classifying all discrete Lie
subgroups of a given Lie group, which is itself a field of study.
Therefore, Lie groups enter into this story quite naturally!

The same reduction applies in the complex category, as summarised in the following diagram.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{sc-fig-09.pdf}
\end{figure}

When we make the identification $E/G\cong M$, where $E$ is a universal
cover of $M$, one can forget $M$: given a smooth structure on $M$, there
is a unique smooth structure on $E$, and hence a unique smooth structure
on $E/G$.

Now consider complex $1$-manifolds, that is, Riemann surfaces. Simply
connected Riemann surfaces are classified.

\setcounter{theorem}{4}\begin{theorem}[Uniformisation theorem]\label{thm:uniformisation}
Given a simply connected Riemann surface $S$, we have
$S\cong\mathbb C$, $\mathbb P_1$, or $\mathbb D$.
\end{theorem}

If $G<\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb P_1)$ acts freely and properly discontinuously on
$\mathbb P_1$, then $G=\{1\}$. In the case of $\mathbb C$,
$G=\{1\}$, $G\cong\mathbb Z$, or $G\cong\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$. These are easy
consequences of elementary theorems taught in a complex analysis or
topology course.

Now it merely remains to classify the discrete subgroups of the Lie
group $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb D)\cong\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$.
