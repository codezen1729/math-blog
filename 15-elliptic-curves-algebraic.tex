\section*{Elliptic Curves — Algebraic Perspective}
% BLOG-CONTENT-BEGIN
\textbf{II) \underline{Algebraic Perspective}}

A plane cubic curve is the following set,
$$C = \big\{[x:y:z]\in \mathbb{CP}^2  \big|   F(x, y, z) = 0,   \text{deg}(F) = 3,   F\text{ is homogeneous}\big\}$$
Let $P\in C$ be one of the 9 points of inflection, and $Q$ be a point on the tangent of $C$ at $P$. By an affine transformation of variables $x, y, z$, the points $P, Q$ seen as points in $\mathbb{C}^3\backslash \{(0,0,0)\}$ can be sent to points $(0,1,0)$ and $(1,0,0)$. Let the polynomial be, 
$$F(x,y,z) = \sum_{0\leq i, j\leq 3}a_{ij}x^iy^jz^{3-i-j}$$
$[0:1:0] \in C$ implies $x=0$ is a root of the polynomial,
$$a_{30}x^3 + a_{21}x^2y+ a_{12}xy^2 + a_{03}y^3$$
By the way things have been set up, the line $[t:1:0]$ is tangent at $[0:1:0]$. Now as $[0:1:0]$ is an inflection point, the multiplicity of $x=0$ in the polynomial above is $3$, implying that $a_{21} = a_{12} = a_{03} = 0$, and that $a_{30} \neq 0$. Let's now look at $F$ in the affine coordinate $z\neq 0$,
$$F(x,y,1) = y^2 -2(ax+b)y - P_3(x)$$

where the terms in the RHS have been extracted from the monomials $yz^2, xyz, z^2y$, and $x^3$, in that order. Complete the square by sending $y \rightarrow y - ax - b$ to get,
$$y^2 = P_3(x) = (x-a)(x-b)(x-c)$$

to be the zero locus of $F$ in the transformed affine chart. Note that for the curve to be smooth $a, b, c$ must be distinct. Further change the coordinates by sending $x \rightarrow (x+a)/(a-b)$ to get,
$$y^2 = x(x-1)(x-\lambda)$$

which with the transformation $x \rightarrow x + (1+\lambda)/3$ becomes,
$$y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$$

The above is called the \textbf{Weierstrass normal form} of the cubic curve, which when graphed would look like:-

            \caption{The graph of the curve $y^2 = x^3 - 2x + 1$. It represents the real section of the complex cubic curve in a local chart.}

In the previous section lattices featured in as group of symmetries of a geometric object quite naturally. We shall now incorporate lattices to illuminate the algebraic perspective. Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}\omega_1\oplus \mathbb{Z}\omega_2$ wih $\text{Im}(\omega_1/\omega_2) >0$ ((i.e.) it is $z \rightarrow z/\omega_2$ away from being a lattice of the form $\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\tau$ in its equivalence class). Define,
$$\wp_{\Lambda} : \mathbb{C} \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$$


$$\wp_{\Lambda}(z) := \frac{1}{z^2} + \sum_{\omega \in \Lambda\backslash \{0\}}\bigg(\frac{1}{(z-\omega)^2} - \frac{1}{\omega^2}\bigg)$$

The object defined above goes by the name \textbf{Weierstrass $\wp$ function}. Let us study its nature to reveal the cameo of lattices for cubic curves; we shall do it in four steps. 

\underline{STEP 1 (Convergence)}:- Note that, 
$$\bigg|\frac{1}{(z-\omega)^2} - \frac{1}{\omega^2}\bigg| = \bigg|\frac{2z\omega - z^2}{\omega^2(z-\omega)^2)}\bigg| = \bigg|\frac{\omega}{\omega^4}\bigg(\frac{2z-z^2\omega^{-1}}{(z\omega^{-1} - 1)^2}\bigg)\bigg| =: \frac{A(z,w)}{|\omega|^3}$$

If $z\in K$ (compact in $\mathbb{C}$), then dist$(K, \Lambda) > \epsilon$, as a result $z\omega^{-1} - 1$ is bounded. For $|\omega|$ large enough note that $A(z, w) \sim 2z$, and as $z\in K$, we see that $A(z, w)$ is uniformly bounded on $K$. Hence, 

$$\bigg|\frac{1}{(z-\omega)^2} - \frac{1}{\omega^2}\bigg| = \mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\omega|^3}\bigg)$$

Now, 

$$\sum_{\omega \in \Lambda\backslash \{0\}} \frac{1}{|\omega|^3} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Bigg(\sum_{\text{max}(|p|, |q|) = n} |p\omega_1 + q\omega_2|^{-3}\Bigg) \leq \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty}\frac{8n}{nh^3}$$


where $h$ is the smaller of the heights of the parallelogram generated by $0$, $\omega_1$, $\omega_2$, and $\omega_1+\omega_2$ (called the \textbf{fundamental parallelogram} of $\Lambda$). The last inequality above is illustrated below.

\begin{figure}[htp]
            \centering
            \includegraphics[width=15cm]{Inequality.jpg}
            \caption{The colors represent the sequence in which the double sum is carried over, and note that there are 8 blue dots, 16 orange dots, and 24 pink dots, and so on.}
        \end{figure}


Now as uniform limit of holomorphic functions is holomorphic, we have $\wp_{\Lambda}(z) \in \mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}\backslash\Lambda)$ with poles of order $2$ on $\Lambda$.

\underline{STEP 2 (Periodicity)}:- Clearly, 
$$\wp_{\Lambda}'(z) = \frac{-2}{(z-\omega)^3}$$

has periods $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$. So does, 
$$\wp_{\Lambda}(z) = \frac{1}{z^2} + \sum_{\omega \in \Lambda\backslash \{0\}}\bigg(\frac{1}{(z-\omega)^2} - \frac{1}{\omega^2}\bigg)$$

\underline{STEP 3 (Zeroes and Poles)}:- Note that $\wp_{\Lambda}(z)$, $\wp_{\Lambda}'(z)$ being doubly periodic factor down to a ramified cover $\mathbb{C}/\Lambda \rightarrow \mathbb{CP}^1$ of degree $2, 3$, respectively. Now as $\wp_{\Lambda}'(z)$ is odd we get,

$$\wp_{\Lambda}'(\omega_i/2) = -\wp_{\Lambda}(-\omega_i/2) = -\wp_{\Lambda}'(\omega_i - \omega_i/2)$$
which implies, 

$$\wp_{\Lambda}'\bigg(\frac{\omega_1+\omega_2}{2}\bigg) = \wp_{\Lambda}'\bigg(\frac{\omega_1}{2}\bigg) = \wp_{\Lambda}'\bigg(\frac{\omega_1}{2}\bigg) = 0$$

These are the only zeroes of $\wp_{\Lambda}'$ since it induces a $3$-fold ramified cover. Let,
$$e_1 = \wp_{\Lambda}'\bigg(\frac{\omega_1}{2}\bigg),   e_2 = \wp_{\Lambda}'\bigg(\frac{\omega_1+\omega_2}{2}\bigg),   \text{and }e_3 = \wp_{\Lambda}'\bigg(\frac{\omega_2}{2}\bigg)$$

By Riemann-Hurwitz formula $\{e_i\}_i$ and $[\Lambda]$ are the only branch points of the $2$-fold cover that $\wp_{\Lambda}$ induces with ramification index $2$ for their preimages (as the derivatives vanishes at those points); which would force them to be distinct.

\underline{STEP 4 (Differential Equation)}:- Consider,

$$H(z) = \frac{\wp_{\Lambda}'(z)}{(\wp_{\Lambda}(z) - e_1)(\wp_{\Lambda}(z) - e_2)(\wp_{\Lambda}(z) - e_3)}$$

Then $H$ is a doubly periodic entire function, meaning $H(z) = c$. Note, 
$$\wp_{\Lambda}(z) = \frac{1}{z^2} + \cdots \text{ and } \wp_{\Lambda}'(z) = \frac{-2}{z^3} + \cdots$$
implying $c = 4$. Therefore, 
$$\wp_{\Lambda}'(z)^2 = 4(\wp_{\Lambda}(z) - e_1)(\wp_{\Lambda}(z) - e_2)(\wp_{\Lambda}(z) - e_3)$$

With the help of basic properties of the Weierstrass function expounded above, we construct the following the map:-

$$\Phi : \mathbb{C} \rightarrow \mathbb{CP}^2$$


$$\Phi(z):= \begin{cases}
[\wp_{\Lambda}(z):\wp_{\Lambda}'(z):1], & \text{ if } z \notin \Lambda\\
[0:1:0], & \text{ else }
\end{cases}$$
Clearly, $\Phi$ factors through the complex torus to yield $\widetilde{\Phi}$,
\[\begin{tikzcd}
	{\mathbb{C}} && {\mathbb{CP}^2} \\
	& {\mathbb{C}/\Lambda}
	\arrow["\Phi", from=1-1, to=1-3]
	\arrow["\pi"', from=1-1, to=2-2]
	\arrow["{\widetilde{\Phi}}"', from=2-2, to=1-3]
\end{tikzcd}\]

The map $\widetilde{\Phi}$ is clearly an analytic embedding with the image in the smooth cubic curve. Indeed, the map near $0$ is given by $$z \rightarrow \bigg[\frac{\wp_{\Lambda}(z)}{\wp_{\Lambda}'(z)}:1:\frac{1}{\wp_{\Lambda}'(z)}\bigg]$$

proving its analyticity at $0$, and the inverse image of $[0:1:0]$ in $\mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ is $[\Lambda]$ establishing that it's a $1$-fold ramified cover, ((i.e.) an embedding). Therefore, lattices parameterize smooth cubic curves.
