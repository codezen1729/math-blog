\section*{Mean, Maximal, and Pointwise Ergodic Theorems}
% BLOG-CONTENT-BEGIN
In our attempt to get a hold on the nature of $\{T^{\circ n}(x)\}_n$ or
$\{T^{\circ n}(E)\}_n$, we saw that $\{T^{\circ n}(x)\}_n$ is recurrent.
Further, if $T$ is ergodic, then $\{T^{\circ n}(x)\}_n$ is recurrent on all
positive-measure subsets of $X$ (for almost every $x\in X$). The next natural
question to ask is: how recurrent are they? Mathematically speaking, we ask
what happens, as $N\to\infty$, to
\[
  A_N^f:=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U_T^n f
  \qquad\text{(the $T$-average of $f$).}
\]
Although we started off by asking about $U_T^n f$ as $n\to\infty$, we shall
see that, as a consequence of the Koopman--Neumann lemma for positive bounded
sequences, if $A_N^f\to A$ as $N\to\infty$, then $U_T^n f\to A$ as
$n\to\infty$, $n\notin J$, where $J$ has ``density'' zero in $\mathbb Z$.
% Author check: this implication is preserved as stated in the handwritten source.
In answering what happens to $\frac1{N+1}\sum_{n=0}^{N}U_T^n f$ as
$N\to\infty$, ergodic theorems will, in some sense, establish a duality
between space and time. We plan to prove the following.

\setcounter{theorem}{8}\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff]
Let $(X,\mathcal B,\mu,T)$ be a measure-preserving system, with $T$ ergodic.
If $f\in L^1_\mu$, then
\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}U_T^j f=\int f\,d\mu.
\]
\end{theorem}

% Source page 2
The left-hand side is an average over time, and the right-hand side, being
an integral, is an average over space. If $f=\chi_B$, then the ergodic
theorem is telling you that the probability of $T^n(x)\in B$ is given by
the size of $B$ (or the measure of $B$). The independence of the theorem
from $x\in X$ is the remarkable thing to observe, and the imposition that
$T$ be ergodic is essential; otherwise, there may be $x\in X$ such that
$T^n(x)\notin B$ for all $n\geq1$. But as soon as you remove that
set-theoretic hindrance, recurrence of orbits is governed by the sizes of
sets. Now let me explain the duality.

Thus certain spatial information can be translated into temporal information,
and vice versa.

% Source page 3
In this lecture we shall give a complete proof of Birkhoff's ergodic theorem
(often also called the pointwise ergodic theorem). We must emphasise that
ergodic theorems are unitary properties.

\setcounter{theorem}{9}\begin{theorem}[von Neumann's mean ergodic theorem: $L^2_\mu$ version]
\label{thm:l3-mean-l2}
Let $(X,\mathcal B,\mu,T)$ be a measure-preserving system. Let $P_T$ be the
orthogonal projection of $L^2_\mu$ onto
\[
  I=\{g\in L^2_\mu:U_Tg=g\}.
\]
Then, for every $f\in L^2_\mu$,
\[
  \frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U_T^nf\longrightarrow P_Tf
  \quad\text{in the $L^2_\mu$ norm.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let us first begin by finding $I^\perp$. Set
$B=\{U_Tg-g:g\in L^2_\mu\}$, and claim that $B^\perp=I$.
If $f\in I$, then $U_Tf=f$. Hence
\[
 \langle f,U_Tg-g\rangle
 =\langle U_Tf,U_Tg\rangle-\langle f,g\rangle=0,
\]
so $f\in B^\perp$. If $f\in B^\perp$, then
\[
 \langle U_Tg-g,f\rangle=0
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \langle U_Tg,f\rangle=\langle g,f\rangle
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \langle g,(U_T^*-\id)f\rangle=0
 \qquad(g\in L^2_\mu).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \sup_{\|g\|\leq1}|\langle g,(U_T^*-\id)f\rangle|
 =0=\|(U_T^*-\id)f\|.
\]
% Source page 4
Thus $U_T^*f=f$. Since $U_T$ is unitary, $U_Tf=f$, so $f\in I$.
Consequently, $B^\perp=I$ and $L^2_\mu(X)=I\oplus\overline B$.
Write $f=P_Tf+h$, with $h\in\overline B$. It now suffices to prove
$\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U_T^nh\to0$ in $L^2_\mu$.
For $h=U_Tg-g$, with $g\in L^2_\mu$, the telescoping series gives
\[
 \left\|\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U_T^n(U_Tg-g)\right\|_2
 =\frac1N\|U_T^Ng-g\|_2
 \leq\frac{\|U_T-\id\|\,\|g\|_2}{N}
 \leq\frac{2\|g\|_2}{N}.
\]
% Author check: the middle operator norm is U_T-Id in the source, not U_T^N-Id.
Here $U_T$, being an isometry, must have norm $1$. Therefore
$\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U_T^nh\to0$ in $L^2_\mu$, since
$2\|g\|_2/N\to0$ as $N\to\infty$.
% Author check: no separate extension from B to its closure is written in the source.
\end{proof}

\setcounter{theorem}{10}\begin{theorem}[von Neumann's mean ergodic theorem: $L^1_\mu$ version]
\label{thm:l3-mean-l1}
Let $(X,\mathcal B,\mu,T)$ be a measure-preserving system. For every
$f\in L^1_\mu$,
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U_T^nf\longrightarrow f'\in L^1_\mu
 \quad\text{in $L^1_\mu$,}\qquad U_Tf'=f'.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Note that
\[
 \left(\int_X|f|\,d\mu\right)^2
 \leq\left(\int_X|f|^2\,d\mu\right)
       \left(\int_X1^2\,d\mu\right),
 \qquad\text{so }\|\cdot\|_1\leq\|\cdot\|_2.
\]
If $g\in L^\infty_\mu\subset L^2_\mu$, then
$\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U_T^ng\to g'$ in $L^1_\mu$.
Now $\|A_N^g\|_\infty\leq\|g\|_\infty$, and
$|\langle A_N^g,\chi_B\rangle|\leq\|g\|_\infty\mu(B)$.
Since $A_N^g\to g'$, the dominated convergence theorem gives
$|\langle g',\chi_B\rangle|\leq\|g\|_\infty\mu(B)$.

% Source page 5
If $g'$ does not have bounded essential supremum, one can find sets $B_N$
such that $\mu(B_N)>0$ and $g'|_{B_N}>N$. Then
\[
 N\mu(B_N)<|\langle g',\chi_{B_N}\rangle|
 \leq\|g\|_\infty\mu(B_N),
\]
a contradiction. Thus $g'\in L^\infty_\mu$. As $g\in L^2_\mu$, the
$L^2$ version of von Neumann's ergodic theorem tells us that $g'$ is
$T$-invariant.

Now we exploit the fact that $L^\infty_\mu$ is dense in $L^2_\mu$.
% Author check: the source says L^2 here, although the approximation below is in L^1.
Fix $f\in L^1_\mu$ and $\epsilon>0$. Let $g\in L^\infty_\mu$ satisfy
$\|f-g\|_1<\epsilon$. Since $T$ is $\mu$-invariant,
$\|A_N^f-A_N^g\|_1<\epsilon$. Also, there are $g'$ and $N_0$ such that
$\|A_N^g-g'\|_1<\epsilon$ for all $N\geq N_0$.
By the triangle inequality,
\[
 \|A_N^f-A_{N'}^f\|_1<4\epsilon\qquad(N,N'\geq N_0).
\]
Thus $A_N^f$ is Cauchy in $L^1_\mu$. Since $L^1_\mu$ is complete
(the Riesz--Fischer theorem), $A_N^f\to f'$.

It is now an elementary exercise to show that $f'$ is $T$-invariant:
\[
 U_TA_N^f=\frac{N+1}{N}A_{N+1}^f-\frac1Nf,
\]
and hence
\[
 \|U_TA_N^f-A_N^f\|_1
 =\left\|\frac{N+1}{N}A_{N+1}^f-A_N^f-\frac1Nf\right\|_1
 \leq\|A_{N+1}^f-A_N^f\|_1+\frac2N\|f\|_1.
\]
As $N\to\infty$, $\|f'\circ T-f'\|_1=0$, so $f'\circ T=f'$ almost
everywhere.
\end{proof}

% Source page 6
\begin{remark}
\label{rem:l3-frequency}
If $T$ is ergodic, then, for $f=\chi_B$, we only know so far that the
frequency of orbit visits of $f$ is a fixed number, independent of $x$.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}[Quantitative estimate]
We have $A_N^{\chi_B}=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\chi_B\circ T$.
% Author check: the source omits the power n on T in this particular formula.
As $T$ is $\mu$-invariant,
\[
 \int_X\chi_B\circ T\,d\mu=\int_X\chi_B\,d\mu=\mu(B)
 \qquad\text{(by Litmus Test 2).}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \int_X A_N^{\chi_B}\,d\mu=\mu(B)
 \geq\sqrt{\mu(B)}\,
 \mu\bigl(\{x:A_N^{\chi_B}(x)>\sqrt{\mu(B)}\}\bigr),
\]
and hence
\[
 \mu\bigl(\{x:A_N^{\chi_B}(x)>\sqrt{\mu(B)}\}\bigr)
 \leq\sqrt{\mu(B)}.
\]
Thus the measure of points in $B$ that spend at least a proportion
$\sqrt{\mu(B)}$ of the time in $B$ is at most $\mu(B)^{1/2}$.
\end{remark}
In light of the quantitative remark above, we have a more quantitative
version of the ergodic theorem.

\setcounter{theorem}{11}\begin{theorem}[Maximal ergodic theorem]
\label{thm:l3-maximal}
Let $(X,\mathcal B,\mu,T)$ be a measure-preserving system, and let
$g:X\to\mathbb R$, $g\in L^1_\mu$. Define, for $\alpha\in\mathbb R$,
\[
 E_\alpha:=\left\{x\in X:
 \sup_{n\geq1}\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}g(T^ix)>\alpha\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \alpha\mu(E_\alpha)\leq\int_{E_\alpha}g\,d\mu\leq\|g\|_1,
 \qquad
 \alpha\mu(E_\alpha\cap A)\leq\int_{E_\alpha\cap A}g\,d\mu
 \quad\text{if }T^{-1}(A)=A.
\]
\end{theorem}

% Source page 7
We shall first prove a technical lemma and derive the theorem from it in a snap.
\setcounter{theorem}{12}\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:l3-positive}
Let $U:L^1_\mu\to L^1_\mu$ be a positive linear operator with
$\|U\|\leq1$. Given $f\in L^1_\mu$, set
\[
 f_k=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}U^\ell f,\qquad f_0=0,
 \qquad F_N=\max\{f_n:0\leq n\leq N\}.
\]
Then $\int_{\{x:F_N(x)>0\}}f\,d\mu\geq0$ for every $N\geq1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We have $F_N\in L^1_\mu$, since $F_N\leq\sum_{n=0}^N|f_n|$.
Also, $F_N\geq f_n$ for $0\leq n\leq N$, so positivity and linearity give
$UF_N+f\geq Uf_n+f=f_{n+1}$. Thus
$UF_N+f\geq\max_{1\leq n\leq N}f_n$.
Set $P=\{x:F_N(x)>0\}$. For $x\in P$,
\[
 F_N(x)=\max_{0\leq n\leq N}f_n(x)
       =\max_{1\leq n\leq N}f_n(x),
\]
since $f_0=0$. Hence $UF_N(x)+f(x)\geq F_N(x)$, or
$f(x)\geq F_N(x)-UF_N(x)$. By positivity, $UF_N(x)\geq0$, as
$F_N(x)\geq0$. It follows that
\[
 \int_P f\,d\mu
 \geq\int_P F_N\,d\mu-\int_P UF_N\,d\mu
 =\int_XF_N\,d\mu-\int_PUF_N\,d\mu,
\]
% Source page 8
because $F_N(x)=0$ if $x\notin P$. Therefore
\[
 \int_Pf\,d\mu\geq\int_XF_N\,d\mu-\int_XUF_N\,d\mu
 =\|F_N\|_1-\|UF_N\|_1\geq0.
\]
Here the equality uses positivity, and the last inequality uses $\|U\|\leq1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:l3-maximal}]
Set $f=g-\alpha$ and $Uf:=f\circ T$. This is a unitary positive operator,
so $\|U\|\leq1$. We have
\[
 E_\alpha=\bigcup_{N=0}^\infty\{x:F_N(x)>0\}.
\]
By the lemma,
$\int_{E_\alpha}f\,d\mu\geq0$, so
$\int_{E_\alpha}g\,d\mu\geq\alpha\mu(E_\alpha)$.
To obtain the other inequality, look at
\[
 \bigl(A,\mathcal B|_A,\mu(A)^{-1}\mu|_A,T|_A\bigr).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This theorem has essentially removed the dependence on $N$ in the
statement made in the quantitative remark above.
\end{remark}
Let us finally prove our desired theorem.

\setcounter{theorem}{13}\begin{theorem}[Birkhoff's ergodic theorem]
\label{thm:l3-birkhoff}
If $f\in L^1_\mu$, then
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^j(x))=f^*(x).
\]
The function $f^*$ is $T$-invariant, and $\int f^*\,d\mu=\int f\,d\mu$.
\end{theorem}

% Source page 9
\begin{proof}
Of course, the measure-preserving system $(X,\mathcal B,\mu,T)$ is lurking
in the background. Since the nature of the result is purely additive, we
may assume without loss of generality that $f:X\to\mathbb R$. Define
\[
 f^*(x)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^i(x)),
 \qquad
 f_*(x)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^i(x)).
\]
\paragraph{Trick (which we noticed before).}
\[
 \frac{n+1}{n}\left(\frac1{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^nf(T^i(x))\right)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^i(T(x)))+\frac1nf(x)
 \leq f^*\circ T+\frac1nf(x).
\]
% Author check: the finite-average comparison with the limsup is written this way in the source.
Choose $\{n_k\}_k$ such that $A_{n+1}^f\to f^*$.
Since $(n_k+1)/n_k\to1$, we obtain $f^*\leq f^*\circ T$.
% Obvious index typography: source's n_k+1 over n_k is read as (n_k+1)/n_k.
If we instead choose $\{n_k\}_k$ such that $A_n^f$ on the right-hand side
converges to $f^*$, then
\[
 \limsup_{n_k}\frac{n+1}{n}A_{n+1}^f
 =f^*\circ T\leq\limsup_n A_{n+1}^f=f^*.
\]
Consequently, and similarly for $f_*$,
\begin{equation}
 f^*=f^*\circ T,\qquad f_*=f_*\circ T.
 \tag{$*$}\label{eq:l3-invariance}
\end{equation}
Let $\alpha>\beta$, with $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb Q$, and set
\[
 E_\alpha^\beta=\{x\in X:f_*(x)<\beta\text{ and }f^*(x)>\alpha\}.
\]
% Source page 10
By \eqref{eq:l3-invariance}, $T^{-1}(E_\alpha^\beta)=E_\alpha^\beta$.
Also, $E_\alpha\supseteq E_\alpha^\beta$, where $E_\alpha$ is as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:l3-maximal}. That theorem gives
\[
 \alpha\mu(E_\alpha\cap E_\alpha^\beta)
 \leq\int_{E_\alpha\cap E_\alpha^\beta}f\,d\mu,
\]
since $T^{-1}(E_\alpha^\beta)=E_\alpha^\beta$.
Similarly, applying it to $-f$ gives
$\int_{E_\alpha^\beta}f\,d\mu\leq\beta\mu(E_\alpha^\beta)$.
Combining these,
\[
 \alpha\mu(E_\alpha^\beta)
 \leq\int_{E_\alpha^\beta}f\,d\mu
 \leq\beta\mu(E_\alpha^\beta).
\]
Since $\alpha>\beta$, $\mu(E_\alpha^\beta)=0$ for all rational
$\alpha>\beta$. By the density of $\mathbb Q$ in $\mathbb R$,
\[
 \{x:f_*(x)<f^*(x)\}
 =\bigcup_{\substack{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb Q\\\alpha>\beta}}E_\alpha^\beta.
\]
The union has measure zero, so $f_*=f^*$ almost everywhere. Thus
\[
 A_n^f(x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^i(x))\longrightarrow f^*=f_*
 \qquad\text{almost everywhere.}
\]
By the $L^1_\mu$ version of von Neumann's ergodic theorem,
$A_n^f\to f'\in L^1_\mu$ in $L^1_\mu$. A consequence of the
% Source page 11
Borel--Cantelli lemma gives a sequence $n_k\to\infty$ such that
\[
 A_{n_k}^f(x)=:g_{n_k}(x)\longrightarrow f'(x)
 \qquad\text{almost everywhere.}
\]
% Obvious notation typo: the handwritten superscript here looks like g, but preceding averages are of f.
Hence $f^*=f'=f_*$ in $L^1_\mu$, and $f^*$ is $T$-invariant because $f'$
is. Now $g_n\to f^*$ almost everywhere. By the dominated convergence
theorem and $T$-invariance of $\mu$, we also get
\[
 \int f\,d\mu=\int g_n\,d\mu=\int f^*\,d\mu.\qedhere
\]
% Author check: the source invokes DCT without an explicit dominating function.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Final remark]
If $T$ is ergodic, then $f^*$ must be constant, since it is $T$-invariant
(the eigenfunctions of $U_T$ corresponding to $1$ are only the constants).
Thus
\[
 \int f^*\,d\mu=f^*\mu(X)=f^*=\int f\,d\mu,
\]
and we finally get the link between space and time averages:
\[
 \boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(T^i(x))
 =\int_Xf\,d\mu.}
\]
\end{remark}
