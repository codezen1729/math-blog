\section*{Prime Ends and Landing of External Rays}
% BLOG-CONTENT-BEGIN
The starting point is the Riemann mapping theorem.
\setcounter{theorem}{32}\begin{theorem}[Riemann mapping theorem]
For a simply connected domain $U\subset\mathbb C$, there is a biholomorphism $\varphi\colon U\to\mathbb D$, unique after prescribing suitable initial values, namely $\varphi^{-1}(0)$ and $(\varphi^{-1})'(0)$.
\end{theorem}
Nothing in this theorem says that the map extends homeomorphically to $\overline U$. We first sketch Carath\'eodory's work on extension, then interpret it for a monic polynomial $f\colon\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ of degree $d\geq2$, with $J=\partial K$ connected. Equivalently, $\mathcal A(\infty)$ contains no finite critical point of $f$.
\subsection{A sketch of Carath\'eodory's work}
Let $\varphi\colon U\to\mathbb D$ be a conformal isomorphism. For now we avoid hyperbolic geometry: the spherical metric $\rho(z)|dz|$ on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$ captures Carath\'eodory's strategy. Equip both $\mathbb D$ and $U$ with the spherical metric.
% Source page 2
For a square $I^2\subset\widehat{\mathbb C}$, its area is $\iint_{I^2}\rho(x+iy)^2\,dx\,dy$, and the length of a horizontal segment $y=C$ is $\int_I\rho(x+iC)\,dx$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\left(\int_I\rho(x+iy)\,dx\right)^2
\le\left(\int_I1^2\,dx\right)\left(\int_I\rho(x+iy)^2\,dx\right).
\]
Hence $\int_I L(y)^2\,dy=O(\operatorname{Area}(I^2))$. Thinking of $I^2\cong\mathbb D$, we conclude that for almost every radial line $r\mapsto re^{i\theta}$, the limit $\lim_{r\to1}\varphi^{-1}(re^{i\theta})\in\partial U$ exists.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.76\textwidth]{cd8-fig-01.pdf}
\FigureTag{CD8.1}{fig:cd8-01}
\end{figure}
For almost every $\theta\in(0,2\pi]$, this gives a candidate for $\varphi^{-1}(e^{i\theta})$. To ensure continuous extension to the boundary, every sequence $z_j\to e^{i\theta}$ must satisfy $\varphi^{-1}(z_j)\to\varphi^{-1}(e^{i\theta})$.

The idea is to divide $U$ by crosscuts $A\subset U$, with $A\cong(0,1)$, $\overline A\cong[0,1]$, and $|\overline A\cap\partial U|=2$.
% Source page 3
The image $\varphi(A)$ is a crosscut of $\mathbb D$. By the Jordan curve theorem it separates $\mathbb D$ into two components, and pulling these components back by $\varphi$ separates $U$ into two crosscut neighbourhoods $U^+$ and $U^-$.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.51\textwidth]{cd8-fig-02.pdf}
\FigureTag{CD8.2}{fig:cd8-02}
\end{figure}
To prevent failure of boundary continuity of $\varphi$ or $\varphi^{-1}$, use nested crosscut neighbourhoods around boundary points: a fundamental chain $\mathcal N=\{N_j\}_j$ of crosscut neighbourhoods with $N_1\supset N_2\supset\cdots$. If $A_j$ are the corresponding crosscuts, require $\overline A_j\cap\overline A_i=\varnothing$ for $i\ne j$, and $\diam(\overline A_j)\to0$.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.52\textwidth]{cd8-fig-03.pdf}
\FigureTag{CD8.3}{fig:cd8-03}
\end{figure}
Combining measure-theoretic and topological arguments, using $\mathbb H^+$ as the universal cover of $\mathbb D\setminus\{0\}$, Carath\'eodory shows that fundamental chains are a topological invariant. This makes the following natural.
\setcounter{definition}{19}\begin{definition}[Carath\'eodory compactification]
Two fundamental chains $\{N_j\}$ and $\{N_j'\}$ are equivalent if each eventually lies in every member of the other: for every $k$ there are $j,j'$ such that $N_j\subset N_k'$ and $N_{j'}'\subset N_k$. An equivalence class $\mathcal E$ is called a prime end of $U$. The Carath\'eodory compactification is
\[
\widehat U=U\sqcup\{\mathcal E:\mathcal E\text{ is a prime end of }U\}.
\]
\end{definition}
% Source page 4
Clearly $\overline{\mathbb D}\cong\widehat{\mathbb D}\cong\widehat U$. To extend $\varphi$ continuously, it remains to compare the topologies of $\widehat U$ and $\overline U$. Thus Carath\'eodory shows that extension is independent of the particular map and depends entirely on the topology of the domain.
\setcounter{theorem}{33}\begin{theorem}[Carath\'eodory]
A conformal isomorphism $\psi\colon\mathbb D\to U$ extends continuously to a map $\widetilde\psi\colon\overline{\mathbb D}\to\overline U$ if and only if $\partial U$ is locally connected.
\end{theorem}
In our setting the conformal isomorphism is the B\"ottcher map $\phi\colon\mathbb C\setminus K\to\mathbb C\setminus\overline{\mathbb D}$. The sets $\{z:\arg\phi(z)=\theta\}$ are external rays of angle $\theta$. For $\theta\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, let $R_\theta=\{re^{2\pi i\theta}:r>1\}$ in the exterior disc. Set
\[
\gamma(t)=\lim_{r\to1}\phi^{-1}(re^{2\pi it}).
\]
If this exists, we say that $R_t$ lands at $\gamma(t)\in\partial K=J$. Let us translate Carath\'eodory's work into this setting.
% Source page 5
\setcounter{theorem}{34}\begin{theorem}[Landing criterion]\label{cd8:landing}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{itemize}
\item every ray $R_t$, $t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, lands, and $\gamma(t)$ is continuous;
\item $J=\partial K$ is locally connected;
\item $K$ is locally connected;
\item $\phi^{-1}\colon\mathbb C\setminus\overline{\mathbb D}\to\mathbb C\setminus K$ extends continuously to the boundary.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from Carath\'eodory's theorem.
\end{proof}
The next result strengthens the landing criterion.
\setcounter{theorem}{35}\begin{theorem}[Rational rays land]\label{cd8:rational-rays}
Every rational external ray lands. A periodic ray lands at a repelling or parabolic periodic point, while a strictly preperiodic ray lands at a preperiodic point.
\end{theorem}
We need a preparatory lemma that is significant in its own right.
\setcounter{theorem}{36}\begin{lemma}[Snail lemma]
Suppose $p\colon[0,\infty)\to V\setminus\{0\}$, $p(t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$, and $f(0)=0$, with $f(z)=\lambda z+a_2z^2+\cdots$ on $V$. Then
\begin{itemize}
\item $f(p(t))=p(t+1)$ for every $t\ge0$ implies $|\lambda|<1$ or $\lambda=1$;
\item $f(p(t))=p(t-1)$ for every $t\ge1$ implies $|\lambda|>1$ or $\lambda=1$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
% Source page 6
\setcounter{theorem}{37}\begin{lemma}[Preparatory lemma]\label{cd8:preparatory}
If $R_t=f(R_t)$ lands at $z_0$, then $z_0$ is a repelling or parabolic fixed point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Parametrise the ray by $p\colon\mathbb R\to\mathbb C\setminus K$, where $p(s)=z$ is determined by $\log\log|\phi(z)|=s$. This $z$ is unique: the argument is fixed by $R_t$, and the modulus is fixed by $|\phi(z)|=e^{e^s}$. Also $z_0=\gamma(t)=\lim_{s\to-\infty}p(s)$.

Since $\phi(f(z))=\phi(z)^d$, we have $\log|\phi(f(z))|=d\log|\phi(z)|$, and $\log\log|\phi(f(z))|=\log d+\log\log|\phi(z)|$. Therefore $f(p(s))=p(s+\log d)$. The landing point is fixed, and the snail lemma gives $|\lambda|>1$ or $\lambda=1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{cd8:rational-rays}]
First suppose that $t$ is periodic under multiplication by $d$. Choose $q\ge1$ with $d^qt\equiv t\pmod1$, and put
\[
g=f^{\circ q},\qquad D=d^q.
\]
Then $g(R_t)=R_t$. Give $\mathbb C\setminus K$ its Poincar\'e metric using
\[
\mathbb C\setminus K\cong\mathbb C\setminus\overline{\mathbb D}.
\]
The map $\exp\colon\mathbb H^+\to\mathbb C\setminus\overline{\mathbb D}$ is a covering, and $z\mapsto z^D$ lifts to the automorphism $w\mapsto Dw$ of $\mathbb H^+$. Hence $g$ is a local isometry for the Poincar\'e metric.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.54\textwidth]{cd8-fig-04.pdf}
\FigureTag{CD8.4}{fig:cd8-04}
\end{figure}

Recall the parametrisation $g(p(s))=p(s+\log D)$. Then
\[
R_t=p(\mathbb R)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb Z}I_k,
\qquad
I_k=p([k\log D,(k+1)\log D]),
\]
with $g(I_k)=I_{k+1}$. All the segments $I_k$ have the same finite Poincar\'e length. As $k\to-\infty$, they approach the boundary $J$; the comparison between the Poincar\'e metric and the spherical distance to the boundary therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{diam}_{\mathrm{sph}}(I_k)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Let
\[
L_t=\bigcap_{s\in\mathbb R}\overline{p(( -\infty,s])}
\]
be the cluster set of the ray at the Julia set. It is nonempty, compact, and connected. If $\widehat z\in L_t$, choose a sequence of segments $I_{k_j}$ approaching $\widehat z$. Since their spherical diameters tend to zero and $g(I_{k_j})=I_{k_j+1}$, both endpoints of these segments tend to $\widehat z$. Continuity gives $g(\widehat z)=\widehat z$. Thus
\[
L_t\subseteq\operatorname{Fix}(g).
\]
The fixed-point set of $g$ is finite, whereas $L_t$ is connected, so $L_t$ is a single point. Therefore $R_t$ lands. The preparatory lemma, applied to $g$, shows that the landing point is repelling or parabolic for $g$, and hence periodic for $f$.
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=.65\textwidth]{cd8-fig-05.pdf}
\FigureTag{CD8.5}{fig:cd8-05}
\end{figure}

Now let $t$ be rational but not periodic. Some angle $d^mt$ is periodic, so the ray
\[
f^{\circ m}(R_t)=R_{d^mt}
\]
lands at a periodic point $y$. The cluster set $L_t$ is connected and satisfies
\[
f^{\circ m}(L_t)=\{y\}.
\]
Hence $L_t$ is contained in the finite set $(f^{\circ m})^{-1}(y)$. A connected subset of a finite set is a single point, so $R_t$ lands at a preperiodic point.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
We have $g(I_k)=I_{k+1}$. A holomorphic map on a hyperbolic surface is distance-reducing unless it is a covering map. Here $z\mapsto z^D$ on $\mathbb C\setminus\overline{\mathbb D}$ is a covering, so $g$ is a local isometry. Since $g|_{I_k}$ is injective, it maps $I_k$ isometrically to $I_{k+1}$.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}
One can also prove the converse of Theorem~\ref{cd8:rational-rays}.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}
The classification of hyperbolic self-maps, together with Sullivan's no-wandering theorem and the snail lemma, gives Sullivan's classification of Fatou components for rational maps.
\end{remark}
